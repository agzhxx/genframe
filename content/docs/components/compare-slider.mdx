Lay a result over its draft, or night over day, and let people slide between them. The after
layer is drawn over the before layer and clipped at the divider, so neither ever changes size.
Put a video in each layer and they play, pause and seek together.

## Installation

```npm
npx shadcn@latest add @genframe/compare-slider
```

Or by URL:

```npm
npx shadcn@latest add https://agzhxx.github.io/genframe/r/compare-slider.json
```

The URL form needs the namespace mapped too ([Add the registry](../index.mdx#add-the-registry)):
the slider brings two Genframe items with it, [useReducedMotion](../utilities/use-reduced-motion.mdx)
and the [motion tokens](../utilities/motion-tokens.mdx), and shadcn finds them through
`@genframe`.

It lands in `components/ui/compare-slider/`: the component, its arithmetic (`slider-math.ts`) and
the video sync (`video-sync.ts`). It needs no npm packages.

## Usage

```tsx
import { CompareSlider } from "@/components/ui/compare-slider/compare-slider"

export function Upscale({ draft, final }: { draft: string; final: string }) {
  return (
    <CompareSlider
      className="aspect-video w-full rounded-xl"
      labels={{ before: "Draft", after: "Final" }}
      before={<img src={draft} alt="Draft" className="size-full object-cover" />}
      after={<img src={final} alt="Final" className="size-full object-cover" />}
    />
  )
}
```

The layers fill the slider: give it a size, here an aspect ratio, and let the media cover it. The
two images should have the same aspect ratio and framing, or they won't line up.

The position is how much of `after` shows, from 0 to 100. It grows from the left edge, like the
filled part of a range input (from the bottom edge when `orientation` is `"vertical"`). To keep
it in your own state:

```tsx
const [position, setPosition] = useState(50)

return (
  <CompareSlider position={position} onPositionChange={setPosition} before={draft} after={final} />
)
```

### Two videos

Put a `<video>` in each layer, and the slider keeps them in step. The `before` video is the clock:
while they play, the `after` video plays a little faster or slower until it's within a frame of
the clock, and jumps to it when they're a quarter of a second or more apart; paused, it shows the
clock's exact frame. Play, pause or seek either video, from your own controls, and the slider does
the same to the other. Set the playback rate on the `before` video.

```tsx
<CompareSlider
  className="aspect-video w-full"
  before={<video src={draft} muted loop playsInline className="size-full object-cover" />}
  after={<video src={final} muted loop playsInline className="size-full object-cover" />}
/>
```

Both videos play, so let only one of them have sound. A player that renders a native `<video>`
works the same way; one that keeps its video inside a shadow root isn't found.

## Keyboard

<KeyboardTable keyboard={compareSliderKeyboard} />

The keys work in both orientations. With a pointer, drag the handle or anywhere on the slider; a
click (a tap on a touch screen) moves the divider there, and a double-click puts it back at 50%.
On a touch screen, swipes along the slider move the divider and swipes across it scroll the page.

## Accessibility

- The handle follows the WAI-ARIA [slider pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/slider/):
  `role="slider"`, `aria-valuemin` 0, `aria-valuemax` 100, `aria-valuenow` (how much of `after`
  shows) and `aria-orientation`.
- `aria-valuetext` reads the position with the after layer's label, "60% Final", or "60%" without
  labels.
- Its name is the two labels, "Draft and Final", or "Before and after" without them. Set
  `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` to say what's being compared.
- The labels drawn on the layers are hidden from screen readers, which hear them in the name and
  the value. Give the images alt text that says which is which.
- The handle is in the tab order and shows a focus ring. A click anywhere on the slider moves focus
  to the handle, so the keys work from there.
- The whole slider is the handle's drag area: keep links, buttons and video controls outside the
  layers.

## Motion

- The divider springs to where you click: its `transform` and the after layer's `clip-path` move
  together, over `--genframe-duration-base` with
  [`--genframe-ease-spring`](../utilities/motion-tokens.mdx), which overshoots by 2.8% and settles.
  Keyboard steps spring the same way.
- While you drag, nothing animates: the divider stays under the pointer.
- With reduced motion, the divider goes straight to its new place.
- `clip-path` is the one animated property here that isn't a transform. The after layer is clipped,
  never resized, so moving the divider doesn't lay the page out again; the browser repaints the
  slider and nothing else.
