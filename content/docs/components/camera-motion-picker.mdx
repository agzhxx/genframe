Generation Feed's red car, set in motion by each of eight camera moves, and three more scenes with
fewer. Rest the pointer on a tile, or Tab into the group and use the arrows, and its clip plays. The
pool has two videos here: watch the counts as you cross the tiles. Turn on Reduced motion to get
play buttons instead.

## Installation

```npm
npx shadcn@latest add @genframe/camera-motion-picker
```

Or by URL:

```npm
npx shadcn@latest add https://agzhxx.github.io/genframe/r/camera-motion-picker.json
```

The URL form needs the namespace mapped too ([Add the registry](../index.mdx#add-the-registry)):
the picker brings [useVideoPool](../utilities/use-video-pool.mdx) with
[createVideoPool](../utilities/video-pool.mdx), [useReducedMotion](../utilities/use-reduced-motion.mdx)
and the [motion tokens](../utilities/motion-tokens.mdx), and shadcn finds them through `@genframe`.
It lands in `components/ui/camera-motion-picker/`.

## Usage

```tsx
import { useState } from "react"
import { CameraMotionPicker, type CameraMotionOption } from "@/components/ui/camera-motion-picker/camera-motion-picker"

const moves: CameraMotionOption[] = [
  {
    id: "dolly-in",
    label: "Dolly in",
    description: "The camera moves in toward the subject.",
    previewSrc: "https://media.example.com/moves/dolly-in.mp4",
    poster: "https://media.example.com/moves/dolly-in.webp",
  },
  {
    id: "orbit",
    label: "Orbit",
    description: "The camera circles around the subject.",
    previewSrc: "https://media.example.com/moves/orbit.mp4",
    poster: "https://media.example.com/moves/orbit.webp",
  },
]

export function CameraField() {
  const [move, setMove] = useState("dolly-in")
  return <CameraMotionPicker options={moves} value={move} onValueChange={setMove} name="camera" />
}
```

No move is picked until the user picks one, unless you give `value` or `defaultValue`. Inside a
form, `name` sends the picked move's `id`.

### Clips and the pool

A tile plays its `previewSrc`, muted and looped, over its `poster`, and shows the clip only once
its first frame is painted, so the poster should be that frame. Tiles are 16:9; other shapes are
cropped to fit. Keep clips short and small: a few seconds at about 480 px tall is plenty for a tile.

Tiles borrow their videos from the page's [video pool](../utilities/use-video-pool.mdx), the one
MediaCard and MediaGrid use: put a `VideoPoolProvider` above them, and however many tiles the
pointer crosses, no more videos than the pool holds play or stay loaded at once; the least recently
used one is taken back first. Without a provider, the picker makes a pool of its own.

A tile's clip plays while it has keyboard focus, as it does under a resting pointer, so arrowing
through the tiles plays each move in turn. Focus from a click doesn't start it.

## Accessibility

- The tiles are native radio buttons in a `radiogroup` named "Camera motion" (set `aria-label` or
  `aria-labelledby`), so the browser gives them the WAI-ARIA
  [radio group pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/radio/) and the arrow keys.
- Each radio is named by its move's `label` and described by its `description`, which is shown
  under the name too, for everyone who can't see or play the clip.
- The picked tile has a check mark as well as its color. The poster and the clip are decoration,
  hidden from screen readers.
- With reduced motion, or when the browser refuses to play (iOS Low Power Mode), each tile has a
  play button, "Play preview of Orbit", a toggle with `aria-pressed`. It sits beside the radio, not
  inside its label, where a button can't be. Only the picked tile's is in the tab order, right
  after the group; the others take clicks and taps, and screen readers reach them all.
- Focus shows as a ring around the tile.

## Motion

- A tile's clip plays after the pointer has rested on it for 100 ms, or keyboard focus has, so
  sweeping across tiles doesn't start each one; on a touch screen, after a tap. It fades in over its
  poster once its first frame is painted, over `--genframe-duration-fast`.
- With reduced motion, nothing plays by itself: each tile shows its poster, its name and a play
  button, and a clip you start appears without the fade.
